# 8. The security compliance market (Secfix's world)

You don't need to be a security expert. You need to understand what customers buy and why, because
that's how you design ICPs, signals and messages.

## Vocabulary

| Term | Plain meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Information security | Keeping data and systems safe: confidentiality, integrity, availability |
| Compliance | Proving you follow a set of rules or a standard |
| Framework / standard | The rulebook, e.g. ISO 27001 |
| Control | One specific rule or safeguard, e.g. "laptops have encrypted disks", "access is reviewed quarterly" |
| Policy | A written document saying how the company does something (access policy, incident response) |
| Evidence | Proof a control works: screenshots, logs, settings exports |
| Audit | An independent auditor checks the controls and evidence |
| Certification | The certificate issued after passing an ISO audit (valid 3 years with yearly surveillance audits) |
| Attestation / report | SOC 2 gives a report, not a certificate |
| Risk assessment | Listing what could go wrong, how likely, how bad, and what you do about it |
| ISMS | Information Security Management System: ISO 27001's name for how security is run |
| CISO | Chief Information Security Officer: the head of security |
| Security questionnaire | A long list of security questions a customer sends before buying (hundreds of questions) |
| Pen test | Ethical hackers try to break in, and report weaknesses |
| Continuous monitoring | Checking controls all the time, not once a year (what automation enables) |

## The frameworks

| Framework | Region / industry | What it is | Typical trigger |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ISO 27001 | International, strongest in Europe | Certifiable standard for an ISMS (Annex A lists 93 controls in the 2022 version) | Enterprise customer asks for it |
| SOC 2 (Type I / II) | USA | Audit report against trust criteria; Type II covers a period of months | Selling to US companies |
| TISAX | Automotive (Germany) | Assessment based on the VDA ISA catalogue | Supplying car makers |
| GDPR | EU | Law on personal data | Everyone with EU users |
| NIS2 | EU | Cybersecurity law covering many sectors; management accountability, incident reporting | Company or its customers fall in scope |
| DORA | EU finance | Digital operational resilience for financial entities and their ICT providers | Banks/fintechs and their suppliers |
| ISO/IEC 42001 | International | Management system for AI | AI companies, EU AI Act readiness |

## How the traditional way works (the pain Secfix removes)

1. Hire consultants. 2. Write dozens of policies. 3. Collect evidence by hand in spreadsheets and
screenshots. 4. Chase engineers for proof. 5. Audit. 6. Repeat yearly. **6–12 months**, expensive,
distracting.

## How compliance automation changes it

Connect to cloud, identity, HR and device tools → checks run automatically → evidence is collected
continuously → policies come from templates → experts guide → audit in weeks.

## How this shapes GTM systems

| GTM element | Compliance-specific version |
| --- | --- |
| ICP | Software/fintech/automotive suppliers selling to enterprises, 20–500 staff, EU |
| Signals | Security/compliance hiring, enterprise or bank customer announcements, funding, US expansion, NIS2/DORA scope, "trust page" without a certificate |
| Negative signals | Already certified with a competitor recently |
| Personas | CTO, Head of IT/Security, CEO, CFO, Head of Sales |
| Messaging | Speed (weeks), less engineering time, expert help, closing the enterprise deal |
| Enrichment | Does their website show ISO/SOC badges? Which cloud do they use? |
